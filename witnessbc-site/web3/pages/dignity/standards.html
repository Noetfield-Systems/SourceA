PUBLIC REPORTING POLICY

Dignity requires accuracy.

People deserve to be heard. They also deserve careful evidence handling, privacy, fair replies, and transparent corrections.

Evidence levels

Reported: a first-hand account, not independently verified. Under review: follow-up is actively documented. Response requested: actual dated correspondence is recorded. Corroborated: independent sources support key details. Corrected / closed: a documented change or conclusion is explained.

Fairness and accountability

Separate observations from interpretation, seek comment from relevant parties and do not portray allegations as findings. A systemic claim requires corroboration. Management direction must not be presumed without evidence.

Privacy and retaliation

Do not automatically publish reports or identify private individuals. Protect confidential records, vulnerable sources and people fearing retaliation. Receiving a report does not constitute consent to public identification.

Rights and legal channels

Disrespectful behaviour does not automatically qualify as unlawful discrimination. For BC's protected-ground discrimination framework, see the BC Human Rights Tribunal's services guidance and official filing process. Witness BC does not replace a lawyer or suspend legal filing deadlines.

Corrections

Send the public case URL, challenged statement and relevant evidence to hello@witnessbc.com. Material revisions should include a date and explanation.
